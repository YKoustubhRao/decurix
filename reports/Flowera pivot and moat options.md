# Stop selling desktops; sell verified work

The critics are right about the layer Decurix/Flowera is built on and wrong about the problem it solves. "An AI agent with its own computer" is saturated. Frontier and open-weight models all score 78-86% on OSWorld-Verified, above the ~72% human baseline. OpenAI and Anthropic shipped record-a-demonstration-into-a-skill to paying users in June and July 2026. Microsoft sells a governed Cloud PC per agent with audit and human-in-the-loop at about $0.04 a step. Standalone desktop-agent startups are being acqui-hired and shut down. What is not saturated is **repeatable, provable execution of specific back-office work on GUI-only legacy software, on infrastructure the customer controls, priced per finished unit**. Benchmark competence still decays sharply over repeated and long runs, and the labs' own documentation says to supervise closely. Decurix already owns the unusual parts of that answer: deterministic model-free replay, an escalation ladder that keeps only corrections that worked, approval gates, a redacted screen-level audit trail and a measured autonomy rate. The recommendation is to stop pitching a horizontal "one VM per employee" platform and a handover tool. Instead, become an **outcome-priced operator of GUI-bound, regulated back-office work**, export-first to US and EU mid-market firms, with Indian operators as demonstrators and escalation humans. The moat has to be built on purpose from four things: per-customer decision traces, a published autonomy and incident record that an auditor or insurer accepts, replay economics that token-selling platforms cannot copy, and self-hosting where the labs cannot go. Test it in 90 days in two verticals, US accounting-firm desktop tax and bookkeeping operations and customs brokerage, against explicit kill criteria. No part of this is yet validated with a paying customer, and the 90 days exist to change that.

## The capability layer is commoditised; the reliability layer is not

Model-level computer use stopped being scarce in 2026. On OSWorld-Verified, Qwen3.8 Max reports **86.1%**, Claude Fable 5 85.0%, the open-weight Qwen3.8-27B **84.3%**, and Gemini 3.6 Flash **83% at $1.50/$7.50 per million tokens** ([BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/osworld-verified); [LLM Stats](https://llm-stats.com/benchmarks/osworld-verified)). A year earlier the best system was at 60.76%, below the ~72% human baseline ([XLANG](https://xlang.ai/blog/osworld-verified)). These are provider-reported numbers that depend on scaffolding, but the direction is not in doubt. Distribution has followed capability. Microsoft took Copilot Studio computer use to GA on 13 May 2026, bundled with Windows 365 Cloud PC pools, Key Vault credentials, Purview audit with session replay and human approval via Outlook, at 5 Copilot Credits (~$0.04) per step ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-copilot-studio/computer-use); [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/copilot-studio-computer-use-agents-ga-deep-dive)). For a Windows/M365 shop, that is most of Decurix's feature list sold as a SKU.

The headline Decurix capability has been absorbed too. **OpenAI shipped Codex Record & Replay on 18 June 2026**: demonstrate a macOS workflow once and get an editable SKILL.md with inputs and verification steps ([OpenAI Developer Community](https://community.openai.com/t/introducing-record-replay/1384088)). **Anthropic followed with "Record a Skill" in Claude Cowork on 21 July 2026** ([Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/blog/claude-cowork-record-a-skill-teach-ai-by-demonstration)). Both emit the Agent Skills format, which 25+ tools read, Microsoft Agent Framework included ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/agent-framework/agents/skills)). Decurix's SKILL.md export therefore helps adoption and removes lock-in in the same move. The infrastructure beneath is collapsing in price as well: Browser Use sells browser infrastructure at **$0.02 per hour** ([Browser Use](https://linkedin.com/company/browser-use)), Cloudflare Sandboxes are GA, and the OpenAI Agents SDK treats seven sandbox providers as interchangeable ([Ry Walker](https://rywalker.com/research/modal)). A persistent VM per worker is the same primitive Daytona, E2B Desktop, Windows 365 for Agents and Simular sell. It is not a moat.

The exits confirm it. Anthropic bought Vercept, whose Vy agent drove a remote MacBook, and **shut the product down a month later**. An investor called it "throwing in the towel" ([TechDogs](https://www.techdogs.com/tech-news/td-newsdesk/anthropic-acquires-computer-use-ai-startup-vercept-as-vy-shuts-down)). Orby, the closest analogue to Decurix's "observe, learn, automate" loop, went to Uniphore on undisclosed terms after raising ~$35M ([Uniphore](https://www.uniphore.com/press-releases/uniphore-announces-the-acquisitions-of-orby-ai-and-autonom8/)). Adobe acqui-hired Rilo and closed it immediately ([Beckmann](https://beckmann.ai/en/ai-economy/2026-09/adobe-acquires-rilo)). The horizontal "record once, agent runs it" niche is crowded with funded teams: Sola ($17.5M, a16z) ([a16z](https://a16z.com/announcement/investing-in-sola/)), Automat ($15.5M Series A) ([Automat](https://runautomat.com/blog/automat-raises-15-5m-series-a)), and Minicor (YC), which already sells self-healing deterministic desktop automation on Windows VMs "behind a Citrix login" ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/Pkq-minicor-self-healing-desktop-automations-that-scale)). The incumbent sells the same idea too. UiPath's CEO pitches "deterministic or tokenless automation alongside AI", model-agnostic and governed, to 10,350 customers ([UiPath transcript](https://exa.ai/library/markets/stocks/PATH/earnings/FY2027/Q2/transcript)). The market is not rewarding the pitch as a platform: net new ARR fell from $70M to $49M to $37M over three quarters, and the stock fell ~17% on the latest print ([ts2.tech](https://ts2.tech/en/uipath-stock-falls-16-6-the-math-behind-its-harder-second-half-arr-hurdle/)).

The case against "saturated" is strongest where the evidence is about repetition rather than capability. Across 23,392 episodes, mean pass@1 fell from **76.3% on short tasks to 52.0% on very long ones** ([arXiv 2603.29231](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.29231)). τ-bench's GPT-4o went from 61% pass@1 to **25% pass^8** ([same](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.29231)). The platforms admit the gap in their own documentation. Anthropic says local computer use "may struggle with complex multi-step workflows", keeps it off Team and Enterprise plans, and does not yet capture Cowork activity in audit logs ([Anthropic Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/14128542-let-claude-use-your-computer-in-cowork); [Claude Cowork](https://claude.com/product/cowork)). Google's Gemini computer-use docs say "supervise closely" ([Gemini API](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/computer-use)). Copilot Studio excludes sovereign clouds and cannot enter passwords into Citrix, Java or Electron apps ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/copilot-studio-computer-use-agents-ga-deep-dive)). Buyers fail on control and measurement, not on model IQ. Gartner expects over 40% of agentic projects to be cancelled for "escalating costs, unclear business value or inadequate risk controls" ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027)). Databricks telemetry shows companies with AI governance put **12x more projects into production** ([Thoughtworks/Databricks](https://www.thoughtworks.com/content/dam/thoughtworks/documents/e-book/State-of-AI-Agents-2026-020426.pdf)). MIT NANDA found purchased specialised tools succeeded ~67% of the time, against about a third as often for internal builds ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/)).

| Layer | Status (Sep 2026) | What decides it |
|---|---|---|
| GUI competence (models) | Commoditised: 83-86% OSWorld, open weights included | Price per token |
| Sandboxes, browsers, VM per agent | Commoditising: $0.02/hr, 7 interchangeable SDK providers | Price, bundling |

## The handover thesis sells the cheapest part of the stack

"Capture a departing employee's know-how" is a knowledge-capture pitch, and knowledge capture already has well-funded leaders plus a free alternative from the labs. Scribe passed **$100M ARR in April 2026** and now serves "workflow context" to agents through an MCP server ([Scribe](https://scribe.com/library/100-million-arr-press-release)). Skan AI raised $63M in August 2026 on 300%+ growth and sells on-prem observation to 7 of the 10 largest US banks ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/data/skan-ai-raises-63-million-betting-that-watching-how-employees-actually-work-is-the-missing-layer-of-enterprise-ai)). Mimica ([Mimica](https://www.mimica.ai/articles/mimica-secures-26-2-million-in-series-b-funding-to-train-agentic-ai-on-real-enterprise-workflows)) and Guidde ($50M) ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/orchestration/visual-imitation-learning-guidde-trains-ai-agents-on-human-expert-video)) pitch recordings as training for agents, and "Company Brain" is on YC's 2026 Request for Startups ([YC Insight](https://ycinsight.com/company-brain)). The research found no evidence that enterprises budget for "handover" as a separate line item. Workplace recording also carries its own resistance: Meta's employee-capture programme is judged likely unlawful under GDPR ([Reuters via Yahoo](https://uk.news.yahoo.com/exclusive-meta-start-capturing-employee-162745587.html)).

The thesis still contains a real insight. a16z's August 2026 analysis argues that once "clicking the right button is no longer the hard part, it is no longer the moat". What stays durable is "the context and knowledge of the workflow… how to verify output reliably", increasingly captured "in a single recorded video of someone doing the job once" ([a16z](https://a16z.com/can-agents-use-a-computer-yet-weve-got-the-data/)). The operative words are *verify reliably*. The labs made capture free, and verification is still scarce. A handover recording should therefore be how Decurix gets taught a workflow (the intake), not what the customer pays for. The broad "AI employee" framing has a poor record as well. Artisan dropped its "stop hiring humans" campaign at ~$9M ARR ([ARR Club](https://www.arr.club/artisan-ai)), and reporting said only ~$3M of 11x's claimed $14M ARR survived pilot break clauses ([Startup Intros](https://startupintros.com/orgs/11x)). No general "AI worker" company appears among the revenue leaders ([New Market Pitch](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/agentic-ai-top-revenue)).

## Money lands where one unit of work is finished and billed

The companies with real agent revenue share one pattern: they own a single measurable unit of work in one function, price per outcome, carry deployment themselves, and sit inside the system of record. Sierra went from $100M to ~$200M ARR by May 2026 on outcome pricing. Escalations to a human are free, a pre-negotiated rate applies only when the agent resolves the case, and the valuation is $15.8B ([Sierra](https://sierra.ai/blog/100m-arr); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/sierra/)). EvenUp ties case inputs to settlement outcomes, uses 100+ human expert reviewers and is valued at $2B+ ([EvenUp](https://www.evenuplaw.com/blog/evenup-2b-valuation/)). The services-as-software variant has early proof. Crescendo bought a 3,000-person CX outsourcer, reports **60-65% gross margins** (an investor claim) and is EBITDA-positive at $50M+ ARR ([Yahoo/PitchBook](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/technology/articles/general-catalysts-6-3b-amex-230800080.html); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/crescendo/)). Long Lake reached $100M EBITDA in under two years ([Yahoo/PitchBook](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/technology/articles/general-catalysts-6-3b-amex-230800080.html)). Incumbents concede the same point. UiPath and Automation Anywhere now lean on forward-deployed engineers and outcome deals ([AA](https://www.automationanywhere.com/company/press-room/automation-anywhere-reports-largest-outcome-based-transaction-companys-history)), and Genpact is moving from FTE pricing to outcome-linked ARR as its headcount falls while revenue grows ([Genpact](https://media.genpact.com/2026-08-06-Genpact-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results)).

This is where Decurix's architecture stops being a feature list and becomes an economic weapon. a16z puts a model-driven agent at **$6-8 per hour of inference**, against ~$10/hour for offshore BPO and $30-45/hour for US back-office staff, and notes "the harness drives real-world cost as much as the model does" ([a16z](https://a16z.com/can-agents-use-a-computer-yet-weve-got-the-data/)). A taught Decurix skill replays with **no model at all**. It consults a model only on escalation, and routes to a person only when both deterministic healing and the model are unsure. A replay-first operator therefore pays close to zero inference on the happy path. Competitors that bill per step (Microsoft's ~$0.04) or reason per step pay the model on every click. That is the counter-position. Labs and Microsoft make money when models run, so a business that profits from models *not* running is awkward for them to copy. The same property makes air-gapped and CPU-only deployments viable. On the founder's own 8 GB host a cold model load takes minutes, so an air-gapped product that needed a model per step would be unusable, while one that replays deterministically and escalates rarely is not. The margin claim is an inference that has to be measured as cost per completed unit on real workloads. It is also the strongest argument the company has.

India changes the escalation half of the equation. India's BPM sector is ~$59B, and NASSCOM describes revenue decoupling from hiring ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/enterprise-story/2026/02/india-tech-industry-revenue-to-touch-315-billion-in-fy26-nasscom)). Contact-centre BPO was called "uninvestable" by RBC in June 2026 ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-30/call-center-stocks-fall-on-worry-ai-is-makes-them-uninvestible)). An India-based team can staff the "ask a person" rung and the demonstrations at Indian cost while selling at US prices. That is Crescendo's model rebuilt on a cheaper labour base, with a published autonomy rate as the margin metric a services-as-software investor wants. General Catalyst's stated first KPI for such businesses is "tasks processed without human intervention" ([General Catalyst](https://www.generalcatalyst.com/stories/the-future-of-services)).

The vertical has to be one where the system of record is a GUI with no API, where the buyer has a reason to keep data on its own hardware or to demand an audit trail, and where funded GUI-native competition is thin. Against those tests, hospital revenue cycle is a trap despite $43B of annual collections effort ([TechTarget/AHA](https://www.techtarget.com/revcyclemanagement/news/366640113/AHA-Hospitals-spent-43B-chasing-pay-from-denials-prior-auths)). Candid, Latent, Commure, Tennr, Luminai and Anterior have raised $38-120M rounds ([RevCycleAI](https://revcycleai.com/deals-and-raises/)), and CMS-0057-F moves prior authorization to FHIR APIs by January 2027 ([CMS](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/cms-interoperability-prior-authorization-final-rule-cms-0057-f)). Freight is crowded and mostly email and voice (HappyRobot at $1.2B) ([Forkast](https://forkast.news/happyrobot-wants-1-2-billion-to-run-your-freight-by-voice/)). Three wedges pass. The first is **accounting firms' desktop tax and bookkeeping operations**: Lacerte and UltraTax are "desktop-installed software… built over decades" with no open APIs ([SynergyIQ](https://synergyiq.net/blog/blog-cpa-cch-axcess-ultratax-lacerte-scale-limitations.html)), practitioners judged 2026 AI tax prep "not ready" ([WCG](https://wcginc.com/blog/ai-tax-prep-in-2026-not-quite-there-yet/)), and Basis proves buyers pay, at $1.15B, while aiming at the top-25 firms ([CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/02/24/basis-raises-100-million-to-deploy-ai-agents-for-accounting-firms/178759/)). The second is **customs brokerage**, where every funded AI player found has raised $4.5M or less ([Pax AI](https://www.paxai.com/blog/pax-ai-raises-4-5m-seed-funding-duty-drawback-ai-tariff-refunds)) and brokers already respond to private-deployment pitches ([SupplyChainBrain](https://www.supplychainbrain.com/articles/44567-private-ai-reshapes-customs-brokerage-under-a-regulatory-whiplash)). The third is **GxP life-sciences quality operations**, where the architecture is itself the regulatory unlock. 21 CFR 11.10(e) demands secure, time-stamped, independent audit trails of operator actions ([eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-11/subpart-B/section-11.10)), and FDA's final CSA guidance rewards risk-based validation ([FIVE Validation](https://fivevalidation.com/final-fda-computer-software-assurance-guidance/)). A fixed replay compiled from an approved demonstration can be validated once; a free-roaming agent cannot. The sale is slow, but the moat is deep.

## The recommendation: an outcome-priced operator with four deliberate moats

Decurix should become **the operator that finishes a named unit of GUI-bound back-office work (a return prepared to review, an entry filed, a reconciliation closed) at a published autonomy rate, on the customer's own infrastructure, priced per finished unit**. The first market is US mid-market accounting firms' desktop tax and bookkeeping operations, with customs brokerage tested in parallel and GxP quality operations as the high-moat expansion once the company can afford a validation sale. Each customer workflow is taught through a demonstration: the departing-employee handover becomes the onboarding ritual. It replays without a model, escalates to Indian operators when unsure, and every run produces evidence. The product is still the Decurix stack. What changes is what is sold, to whom and how it is priced.

None of Decurix's current parts is a moat on its own, and the 2026 consensus is explicit that prompts, wrappers, generic RPA execution, fine-tuned models and static datasets have eroded ([Kaveti](https://skaveti.substack.com/p/the-saaspocalypse-how-claude-cowork); [Howardism on 7 Powers](https://www.howardism.dev/articles/seven-powers-applied-to-ai)). The moats that are holding are outcome-labelled feedback loops embedded in the work, counter-positioned pricing, cornered trust assets and sovereignty. Each maps onto something Decurix can build deliberately.

The first moat is **decision traces that cannot be reconstructed later**. Foundation Capital's context-graph thesis argues that whoever sits "in the execution path at commit time" accumulates records of what was done, who approved it, what was corrected and what happened. Incumbents cannot reconstruct those records, and precedent lets autonomy "ratchet" ([Foundation Capital](https://foundationcapital.com/ideas/context-graphs-ais-trillion-dollar-opportunity)). Decurix already keeps a correction only if the run then succeeded, promotes repairs after repeated confirmation and flags fragile skills. Those are the ratchet mechanics. What is missing is tying the record to business objects (returns, entries, invoices) rather than to screens. FC concedes the objection that "you can't capture the why", and no company has yet shown decision-trace accumulation producing measurable retention ([FC](https://foundationcapital.com/ideas/context-graphs-one-month-in)). Treat this moat as a hypothesis to measure, not an assumption.

The second moat is **counter-positioned economics**, covered above. The goal is to be paid for outcomes while spending almost nothing on inference, which seat-priced SaaS and token-priced platforms find structurally awkward. Outcome pricing is exposed where buyers benchmark it against labour: Intercom Fin fell from $1.90 to $0.99 per resolution ([Value Add VC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/decagon-valuation-2026-4-5b-35m-arr-ai-customer-service-boom)). So the cost advantage has to be structural (model-free replay plus Indian escalation), not a pricing tactic.

The third moat is **evidence an outside party relies on**. Insurers say their binding constraint is underwriting data, not capital ([agentinsured.eu](https://agentinsured.eu/articles/european-ai-agent-insurance-market-tracker-june-2026)). Underwriters ask for oversight records, incident logs and a statement of human approval gates ([Agent Insured](https://agentinsured.eu/articles/what-ai-insurance-underwriters-ask-before-writing-policy)). AIUC-1 recertifies quarterly through accredited auditors such as Schellman ([aiuc-1.com](https://www.aiuc-1.com/)), and Armilla's cover explicitly includes "escalation errors" ([Beinsure](https://beinsure.com/news/armilla-ai-expands-standalone-ai-liability-cover-to-25m/)). The research found no product that proves what a computer-use agent did on screen, with before/after frames, the resolved target, whether a gate fired, and an outcome class that separates "verified" from "completed_unverified" from "reconciliation_required". Decurix's outcome taxonomy already does exactly that. If an insurer or certifier accepts Decurix's per-workflow autonomy and incident history as the number it prices on, that history becomes a cornered resource. That has not happened yet.

The fourth moat is **sovereignty where the labs structurally are not**. Codex Record & Replay launched without the EEA, UK and Switzerland ([OpenAI community](https://community.openai.com/t/introducing-record-replay/1384088)), Cowork's actions are not in Anthropic's audit logs, and Copilot Studio skips sovereign clouds. The strongest public evidence that private deployment pays is Cohere: **85% of its reported $240M ARR comes from on-prem and VPC deployments**, and its North agent platform runs air-gapped ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/13/ai-startup-cohere-revenue-ipo.html); [FNEX](https://fnex.com/coheres-240m-arr-highlight/)). Palantir's 2026 pitch, that a customer's alpha must not become lab training data, is nearly Decurix's architecture ([Palantir Q2 call](https://transcripts.platformaeronaut.com/transcripts/PLTR-2Q26-transcript)).

The billion-dollar path runs through revenue, not architecture. At Crescendo's ~10x ($500M on $50M+ ARR), a $1B outcome needs about $100M of revenue at software-like margins. At the multiples vertical agent leaders command (Harvey ~39x ARR ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/harvey-550m-round-15-6bn-guardrails-acquisition))), it needs far less. As an illustration only, 500 mid-market firms at $150-200K a year each is roughly $75-100M. That is a plausible ceiling for one vertical and a reason to plan the second (customs, then GxP) early. Two engineering gaps sit on the critical path, both visible in the codebase rather than the market research. First, tax and broker software is Windows-only, and Decurix today shows Windows apps by running them on a Windows *host*, while `visual.py` has been tuned only on Chromium pixels. A Windows guest or Citrix-client path with measured pixel grounding is prerequisite zero. Second, the control plane holds the Docker socket, which is root on the host, and any regulated buyer's security review will flag it before anything else.

Competition in the lead vertical is real and should be named. Basis owns the top of accounting, and Thrive Holdings' Crete Professionals Alliance is rolling up accounting firms with OpenAI engineers embedded ([Newcomer](https://www.newcomer.co/p/inside-the-vc-roll-up-craze-that); [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260202196878/en/Shield-Raises-$100-Million-From-Thrive-Holdings-to-Accelerate-the-Growth-of-its-IT-Services-Platform)). The same roll-ups are also candidate *customers*, because an operator buying firms needs exactly the automation and autonomy metric Decurix produces. Two things remain unverified: whether Basis or Juno drive Lacerte/UltraTax GUIs directly or rely on import files, and whether small firms will pay extra for keeping data on their own hardware.

## Four alternatives, and what to stop

The alternatives are credible, and three of them make good bridges even if none is the best main bet.

| Alternative | Time to first revenue | The $1B version | Why it ranks below the recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Evidence and certification layer** ("flight recorder" for computer-use agents, sold to agent vendors, auditors, insurers) | 3-6 months with one design partner | SOC 2-style evidence layer mandated by insurers and standards, as Vanta rode SOC 2 | Narrow. Consolidators are buying adjacent tools (Dynatrace–Arize $915M ([Dynatrace](https://ir.dynatrace.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/435/dynatrace-to-acquire-ai-observability-leader-arize)), Cisco–Galileo), so it likely ends as a feature or acquisition unless a standard requires screen-level telemetry |
| **B. Self-hosted desktop-automation infrastructure for vertical AI startups** ("Minicor for regulated/on-prem") | 3-6 months | Picks-and-shovels under the well-funded RCM, freight and insurance agents | Platform sale to startups. Minicor already occupies the cloud version, and the buyers can in-source it |
| **C. Desktop RL environments and verified task packs for labs** | Fastest *if* a relationship exists: "six to seven figures per quarter" ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/state-of-rl-envs)); ~$20K per UI-gym site, ~$300K for a complex clone, $200-2,000 per task ([SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/rl-environments-and-rl-for-science)) | Environment factory for enterprise desktop software | Most vendors serve 1-3 customers, labs are in-sourcing, foundries acquire builders (Mercor–Deeptune ([RuntimeWire](https://runtimewire.com/article/mercor-20b-valuation-talks-deeptune-acquisition))), and video pretraining is eroding the value of demonstrations |
| **D. Sovereign agent platform for Indian and Gulf regulated banks**, sold through a systems integrator | 9-18 months | Cohere North for GUI-bound work | RBI's model-risk guidance (June 2026) and DPDP (full obligations May 2027) are real forcing functions ([AccuKnox](https://accuknox.com/blog/rbi-model-risk-management-ai-security); [AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-phased-rollout-and-key-compliance-milestones/)), but the sale is long, needs certifications and HA, and core vendors (Fiserv agentOS, FIS with Anthropic) are claiming the back office |

Option C deserves to run alongside the main bet as bridge revenue. `bench/gui` already has the right shape: dockerised desktop, task, deterministic outcome check and full telemetry. Micro1's data shows reusable non-exclusive packs earn 80-90% gross margin ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/20/ai-data-startup-micro1-reaches-500m-gross-run-rate-amid-ai-training-boom/)). Option A should not be a separate company. It should be the packaging of the main business's exhaust, taken to one auditor and one insurer. Buying or partnering with a mid-sized Indian BPO to convert its staff's work (the Crescendo–PartnerHero pattern) is attractive, but it needs a sponsor such as General Catalyst or Thrive, and no precedent for doing it with an Indian BPO was found ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/adobe-acquires-indian-ai-automation-startup-rilo-to-boost-agentic-push-126090201601_1.html)). Keep it as a Series A option, not a 90-day move.

The things to stop follow from the evidence. **Stop pitching "one VM per employee" or an "AI employee" platform**: it is the saturated primitive, and the AI-employee category has the weakest revenue record in agents. **Stop leading with handover**, and keep the recording as intake, because Scribe, Skan and the labs own the capture story. **Stop treating three engine harnesses, and TheBot in particular, as the differentiator.** Engine-agnosticism is table stakes (UiPath and Harvey both went multi-model), and the codebase's own notes call TheBot the slow harness on memory-starved hosts. **Stop building horizontal conveniences** that Microsoft and the labs bundle (schedules, email triggers, DAGs, keep-awake, broad Windows host-app bridging), and put that effort into Windows-guest/Citrix grounding, repeated-run reliability and the evidence pack. **Stop making local CPU inference the default experience**: model-free replay is what makes air-gap work, and demos should show replay, not a 3B model thinking for minutes. Finally, **stop treating Indian SMB and CA-firm tooling as a revenue base.** Price points around $29-99 a month and crowded local tools ([Omago, weak source](https://www.omago.ai/blog/sme-ai-adoption-2026-data)) make it a proving ground at best.

## Ninety days, two verticals, explicit kill criteria

The 90 days should produce decision-grade evidence on four open questions. Will a mid-market firm pay per finished unit? Does replay hold up over repeated runs on real Windows/Citrix apps? Is self-hosting a reason to buy or just a nice-to-have? Will anyone outside engineering rely on the evidence? The thresholds below are proposals calibrated to the comparables (Crescendo claims ~90% autonomous resolution, while GC's Titan showed 38% task automation ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/28/the-ai-services-transformation-may-be-harder-than-vcs-think/))). They are not industry standards.

| Days | Action | Evidence that counts |
|---|---|---|
| 1-21 | 40 discovery calls: ~25 US accounting firms (50-500 staff, desktop tax on Citrix/RDS, especially firms already offshoring prep work) and ~15 customs brokers. Ask for current cost per return/entry/reconciliation, where client data may live, and whether they would pay per finished unit | ≥8 firms name a workflow and a current unit cost; ≥5 say data must stay on hardware they control |
| 1-30 | Engineering spike: run one target app (UltraTax/Lacerte or the brokers' ABI software) in a Windows guest VM or via a Citrix client inside the desktop VM; extend the visual-anchor corpus to its pixels; remove the raw Docker socket from the customer install path | One demonstrated workflow replays 50 consecutive times with outcome checks, reported as pass^k, not pass@1 |
| 21-60 | 2-3 paid pilots, outcome-priced, with Indian operators as demonstrators and escalation humans, using the handover recording as intake | Signed pilot fees; per-unit autonomy rate, human minutes per unit and cost per unit against the customer's baseline |
| 30-75 | Take one pilot's evidence pack (approval log, before/after frames, outcome classes, incident log) to an AIUC-1 auditor (Schellman) and an agent insurer (Armilla, or Mosaic for Munich Re aiSure) | A written view on whether screen-level evidence counts toward certification or underwriting |
| 30-90 | Bridge: offer a verified desktop/office task pack built on `bench/gui` to one neolab or data foundry | A priced LOI, non-exclusive, that does not constrain enterprise work |
| 90 | Decide | See below |

**Go** on the operator model if at least two pilots are paying, one workflow reaches **≥60% of units finished with no human touch across 50+ repeated runs** and is trending up, cost per finished unit is **≤40% of the customer's current cost**, and at least one buyer names self-hosting or the audit trail as the reason to choose Decurix over Basis, Copilot Studio or a cloud agent. Then pick whichever of accounting or customs cleared the bars more convincingly, and raise on the autonomy curve. If demand is real but nobody values self-hosting, keep the operator model and drop sovereignty as a pillar; compete on outcome price and evidence instead. If pilots stall below ~40% autonomy but the auditor or insurer engages seriously, pivot to Alternative A with the pilot data as its first asset. If both fail and only the lab LOI lands, take C as bridge revenue while reassessing. That is survival, not a path to $1B.

## Conclusion

The most useful reframing is that Decurix's value sits on the opposite side of the model from where the market is crowding. Everyone else is racing to make models run more of the computer. Decurix's distinctive code (deterministic replay, heal-before-asking, keep-only-what-worked, never claim a success it did not see) is about making models run *less* while proving more. That is a poor product for a lab-dominated horizontal market and a strong one for a business that is paid per finished unit and profits when no model is called. The handover thesis was not wrong about where durable value lives. It aimed at capture, which the labs made free, instead of verification, which nobody has made cheap.

The honest risk is that every moat named here is a design intent, not an observed fact. No customer has yet paid per outcome, no insurer has accepted the evidence, and repeated-run reliability on Windows/Citrix tax or broker software is unmeasured. The founder should therefore treat the next quarter as a measurement exercise, and let pass^k, cost per unit and one signed outcome price decide the company's shape. A competitor can copy the architecture. The dated record of runs, corrections and approvals accumulated inside a customer's own infrastructure cannot be copied later, which is why starting to accumulate it now matters more than any single feature.

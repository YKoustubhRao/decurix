# Market saturation and value capture in computer-use agents, browser agents, agent sandboxes, AI RPA and "AI employee" products (as of 24 Sep 2026)

Scope note: every figure is dated. Vendor-reported metrics (ARR, ACV, "reliability") are labelled as such. Several aggregator or low-quality sources turned up in search. Where one is used it is flagged, and it is never the only support for a load-bearing claim. The research date is 24 Sep 2026.

## 1. Funding and M&A 2024-2026: amounts, valuations, acquirers, shutdowns and pivots. Which categories are commoditising fastest?

### Takeaway
Capital is abundant but very concentrated. Generic computer-use and desktop-agent startups raised small rounds ($2.7M-$27M) and are being absorbed or shut down by labs and platforms (Vercept to Anthropic, Orby to Uniphore, Manus to Meta and then reversed). Sandbox and browser infrastructure is a real category, but it is visibly commoditising: price cuts to about $0.02 per browser-hour, Cloudflare Sandboxes going GA, and seven sandbox providers plugged into the OpenAI Agents SDK. The one breakout there is Modal, a general compute platform whose sandboxes are about a third of revenue. The largest cheques go to agent companies that own a measurable vertical workflow (Sierra, Decagon, Factory, Ema) or a governance/control layer (Island).

### Inferences
- Commoditising fastest: (1) **raw sandboxes and headless browsers.** Prices are collapsing to cents per hour, hyperscalers and Cloudflare are entering, SDKs treat providers as interchangeable, and Browserbase is modest (~$4M ARR, 2025 est.) next to its $300M valuation. (2) **Generic computer-use agents / desktop agents.** Lab and platform models do this natively now (section 2), and funded standalone players are being acqui-hired and shut down. (3) **AI SDR "AI employees"** (section 4).
- Value accrues to (a) diversified compute platforms (Modal), (b) governance and control layers (Island at $6.4B; "agent execution infrastructure" funding up ~24x), and (c) vertical workflow owners with measurable outcomes.
- Decurix's "a persistent Linux desktop VM per worker" is, on its own, the same primitive Daytona, E2B Desktop, Cua, Windows 365 for Agents and Simular's "fleet of autonomous computers" all sell. It is not a moat.

### Gaps
- I could not find a disclosed price for Orby–Uniphore, Vercept–Anthropic or the Bytebot acquisition, nor Cua's or Mimica's 2025-26 funding (search returned no reliable figure).
- There is no reliable, methodology-disclosed total for "computer-use agent" funding specifically; aggregator totals conflict.
- I found no reliable report of sandbox/browser startup shutdowns in 2026. Commoditisation evidence is pricing and entrants, not failures.

## 2. What frontier labs shipped in computer use by 2026, benchmark levels, and implications for generic "agent that uses a computer" startups

### Takeaway
By mid-2026 computer use is a built-in feature of every frontier model and every big-platform suite. OSWorld-Verified scores (~83-86% at the top, above the ~72% human baseline from the original study) mean raw GUI competence is no longer scarce. That includes open-weight models (Qwen3.8-27B at 84.3%) and cheap ones (Gemini 3.6 Flash at 83% for $1.50/$7.50 per M tokens). What is still scarce is production reliability over repeated runs, governance, audit, and integration into a customer's workflow. Microsoft reached contractual GA for enterprise computer use first (May 2026), bundled with Windows 365 Cloud PCs, Purview audit and human-in-the-loop, which is essentially Decurix's feature list sold as a Copilot Studio SKU.

**Reliability gap (benchmark vs production)**
- τ-bench's pass^k: GPT-4o scored 61% pass@1 but 25% pass^8 on retail tasks. — [arXiv 2603.29231 citing τ-bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.29231)
- A 23,392-episode study found mean pass@1 drops from 76.3% on short tasks to 52.0% on very long tasks across 10 models. "Reliability decay is universal." — [arXiv 2603.29231 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.29231)
- ReliabilityBench: paraphrase perturbations take 96.9% down to 88.1%. Rule of thumb: "if a benchmark reports 90% accuracy, expect 70-80% in production." — [arXiv 2601.06112 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.06112)
- READY framework (Sep 2026) argues deployment should be qualified on reliability under oversight at a given cost, not on autonomous benchmark score. — [arXiv 2609.02095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02095)

### Inferences
- Model-level computer-use capability is now effectively commoditised. Open weights, cheap Flash-tier models, and every hyperscaler SKU all clear the old human baseline. A startup whose core claim is "our agent can use a computer" competes with a free or bundled feature.
- The unsolved layer is repeatability: the pass^k decay, "struggles with complex multi-step workflows" in Anthropic's own docs, and Gemini's "supervise closely". Deterministic replay of a learned workflow with self-healing and escalation is a direct answer to that gap, and it is the same bet UiPath (section 3), Skyvern ("compile-to-code") and Simular ("repeat its own success") are making. So it is defensible reasoning but not unique.
- Microsoft's GA bundle (Cloud PC per agent, Key Vault, Purview audit, HITL, $0.04/step) closely overlaps Decurix's feature list for Windows/M365 shops. Decurix's differentiation has to come from where Microsoft is weak: Linux, self-hosted or air-gapped, local models, engine-agnostic, sovereign clouds (excluded from Copilot Studio GA), and learning from demonstration rather than natural-language instructions.

### Gaps
- I found no independent (non-vendor) measurement of production success rates for Copilot Studio computer use, Nova Act or Cowork.
- I did not retrieve 2026 WebArena or Online-Mind2Web leaderboards in detail (Online-Mind2Web released Aug 2026 per Enhans; its numbers are not captured here).
- I could not find a GA date for Anthropic's computer-use API. A May 2026 third-party piece says "paid-plan beta since Dec 2025"; Anthropic's own help pages still say beta/research preview.

## 3. How incumbents (UiPath, Automation Anywhere, ServiceNow, Salesforce, Microsoft) are responding; UiPath's agentic pivot results, revenue and stock

### Takeaway
Incumbents are monetising agents at billion-dollar run rates on their own installed bases: Salesforce Agentforce >$1.5B ARR, ServiceNow AI >$1B ACV. They buy AI-native startups (Moveworks, Fin, Sana, Cognigy, Aisera) and rent frontier models (Claudeforce). UiPath's pivot is real in product and margin but not yet in growth: ARR +12%, net new ARR slowing, stock down ~17% after Q2 FY27 and ~24% over the following week. Its message, "deterministic automation plus AI, model-agnostic, governed", is almost exactly Decurix's positioning, sold to 10,350 customers.

## 4. Where revenue-generating winners are (actual ARR) in agentic automation, and what they share

### Takeaway
Real agent revenue is concentrated in a few verticals with a verifiable unit of output: coding (Cognition ~$492M disclosed, Factory), customer service (Sierra ~$200M, Decagon ~$100M est., Parloa $50M+), and legal (Harvey ~$350M). The general "AI employee" category (AI SDRs) stalled at roughly $10-25M ARR with churn, ARR-quality controversies and headcount cuts. Winners share four traits: a narrow, measurable outcome; outcome- or resolution-based pricing; heavy deployment help (forward-deployed engineers, except Decagon); and deep integration into systems of record. No pure "general computer-use agent" company appears among the revenue leaders.

### Cited Findings
- Sierra hit $100M ARR in Nov 2025, seven quarters after launch, and ~$200M in May 2026. It raised a $950M Series E at $15.8B (May 2026), ~79x ARR. It uses outcome-based pricing and a forward-deployed, high-touch model. 50% of customers have >$1B revenue and it serves 40%+ of the Fortune 50. — [Sierra blog](https://sierra.ai/blog/100m-arr); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/sierra/); [CryptoBriefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/sierra-200m-annualized-revenue-may/)
- Decagon: ~$35M ARR (Oct 2025), then a $4.5B valuation (Jan 2026, $250M Series D, ~128x), then ~$100M annualised (Jul/Aug 2026 per Newcomer/Sacra estimates). Pricing is resolution-based. It deliberately avoids forward-deployed engineers. Retention is not disclosed. — [Value Add Pulse](https://valueaddvc.com/pulse/decagon-100m-arr-ai-customer-agents-2026); [Value Add VC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/decagon-valuation-2026-4-5b-35m-arr-ai-customer-service-boom)
- A strict ranking of independent agent-first startups (aggregator, mixed confidence): Cognition ~$900M est. ## 5. Buyer behaviour: pilot-to-production conversion, why agent projects fail, reliability expectations

### Takeaway
Adoption surveys conflict wildly (11% to 75% "in production"), depending on who was sampled and how production is defined. The credible pattern: pilots are near-universal, production at scale is a minority, and failures are organisational (integration, unclear value, governance, measurement), not model capability. Buyers who buy specialised vendor tools convert far better than internal builds. Governance, evaluation and human-review gates correlate strongly with reaching production.

### Cited Findings
- Gartner (25 Jun 2025) predicts more than 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end-2027 due to "escalating costs, unclear business value or inadequate risk controls". The forecast method is not published; the supporting poll was 3,412 webinar attendees. — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027); [Rahim Hirji evidence review (Sep 2026)](https://thesuperskills.com/research/is-it-true-that-95-per-cent-of-ai-pilots-fail)
- Gartner's Jul 2024 prediction (at least 30% of GenAI projects abandoned after PoC by end-2025) reportedly came in above half on Gartner's later analysis (secondary report). — [GovernanceAI](https://governanceai.io/blog/why-ai-projects-fail)
- MIT NANDA "GenAI Divide" (Jul/Aug 2025): "95% of organizations are getting zero return", measured as no measurable P&L impact within 6 months. It rests on 52 interviews, 153 conference survey responses and ~300 public initiatives; it is preliminary and not peer-reviewed. Fortune reported larger sample numbers. For custom embedded tools, 20% reached pilot and 5% production, so roughly 25% of actual pilots cleared the bar. Generic LLM chat tools reached ~83% pilot-to-implementation. **Purchased specialised vendor tools succeeded ~67% of the time vs internal builds ~one-third as often.** The cited cause is a "learning gap": tools that don't retain feedback or adapt to workflow. Critics include Kevin Werbach (Wharton) and Paul Roetzer, and NANDA has a commercial conflict (it builds agent infrastructure). — [Fortune (18 Aug 2025)](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/); [NewMR critique (May 2026)](https://newmr.org/blog/myth-number-2-mit-showed-that-95-of-ai-pilots-fail/)
- S&P Global/451 Research (fieldwork Oct-Nov 2024, n=1,006): companies abandoning most AI initiatives rose from 17% to 42% YoY; 46% of PoCs were scrapped before production. IDC/Lenovo (Mar 2025): 88% of PoCs never reach production. — [GovernanceAI](https://governanceai.io/blog/why-ai-projects-fail); [AgentModeAI](https://agentmodeai.com/the-mit-genai-pilot-failure-claim/)
- ETR/Futurum (2026, n=600): only 18.5% rate their AI maturity 4-5 of 5. 24.3% report no ROI yet and 21.5% don't measure ROI at all. Direct financial impact nearly doubled as the primary success metric, while "productivity" fell from 23.8% to 18%. Production rates by use case: employee support 39.2%, enterprise search 34.5%, customer engagement 34.0%. — [Futurum (Sep 2026)](https://futurumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/From-Pilots-to-Production-Why-Agentic-AI-Runs-Through-the-Ecosystem.pdf)
- Databricks platform telemetry: companies using AI governance put 12x more AI projects into production; those using evaluation tools, ~6x. — [Databricks State of AI Agents via Thoughtworks PDF](https://www.thoughtworks.com/content/dam/thoughtworks/documents/e-book/State-of-AI-Agents-2026-020426.pdf)
- Vendor and practitioner surveys (optimistic, self-selected): LangChain (2026) has 57.3% with agents in production (67% at 10k+ employee orgs). Harness/Sapio (Jul 2026, only orgs already deploying) has 75%. Anthropic/Material (late 2025, n=500+): 80% report measurable ROI. — [LangChain](https://www.langchain.com/state-of-agent-engineering); [Harness](https://www.harness.io/the-state-of-agent-dlc-2026); [Anthropic report](https://resources.anthropic.com/hubfs/The%202026%20State%20of%20AI%20Agents%20Report.pdf)
- Pessimistic survey (low-provenance consultancy blog, n=650, Feb-Mar 2026): 78% have agent pilots, 14% at production scale (defined as >50% of target volume with monitoring). Finance/insurance 21%, healthcare 8%. Average pilot stalls at 4.7 months. — [Digital Applied (low provenance)](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-agent-scaling-gap-march-2026-pilot-to-production)
- Another report (unknown provenance): 67% of production agent deployments keep mandatory human review gates; 41% saw a prompt-injection attempt in 2025; 3-4 months to production for a single agent. — [Halkwinds (unknown provenance)](https://www.halkwinds.com/research/ai-agent-adoption-report-2026)
- Amazon's user research: "reliability is the critical factor preventing broader agent adoption in enterprise environments." — [Amazon Science (Dec 2025)](https://www.amazon.science/blog/amazon-nova-act-service)
- Finance & operations is the least-deployed function in one mid-2026 survey (17% production vs 41% customer support). Low-provenance source. — [Aldric Research](https://aldricresearch.com/enterprise-ai-agents-mid-year-2026)

### Inferences
- Buyers are shifting from "can it do the task?" to "can I prove it did it, repeatedly, under control, at a known cost?" Audit trails, approval gates, measured autonomy rates and deterministic replay line up with the documented failure causes (risk controls, unclear value, no measurement).
- The MIT finding that buying specialised vendor tools beats building favours a productised, vertical offering over a general toolkit that the buyer's own team must assemble. That is a caution for Decurix if it sells as a platform.

### Gaps
- There is no rigorous population-level pilot-to-production rate specific to computer-use/UI agents.
- Recent surveys with the most striking numbers (Aldric, Halkwinds, KXN, Digital Applied) have unclear provenance and should not anchor conclusions.

## 6. Honest verdict: which slices are saturated, which are under-served?

### Takeaway
"Computer-use agent" as a generic capability is saturated and commoditised. So are sandbox/browser infrastructure and horizontal AI-SDR "AI employees". Under-served, per the evidence: (1) reliable, repeatable execution of specific back-office workflows on legacy/UI-only systems, with audit and approval, where Microsoft is Windows/M365-centric and the labs' computer use is beta and unaudited; (2) self-hosted, sovereign or local-model deployments; (3) measurement and outcome pricing tied to verified completion. These gaps are real, but UiPath, Automation Anywhere, Microsoft, Skyvern and Simular are all converging on the same "deterministic + agentic" idea. Differentiation has to come from a vertical wedge and deployment model, not from architecture.

### Cited Findings
- Saturated or commoditising: frontier and open models all at 78-86% on OSWorld-Verified ([BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/osworld-verified)); browser infra at $0.02/hour ([Browser Use](https://linkedin.com/company/browser-use)); sandbox features "no longer differentiate" with Cloudflare GA and 7 SDK providers ([Ry Walker](https://rywalker.com/research/modal)); generic desktop-agent startups acqui-hired and shut down ([TechDogs on Vercept](https://www.techdogs.com/tech-news/td-newsdesk/anthropic-acquires-computer-use-ai-startup-vercept-as-vy-shuts-down)); AI SDRs stuck far below $50M ARR ([New Market Pitch](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/agentic-ai-top-revenue)); Gartner counts ~130 real vendors among thousands ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027)).
- Gaps in big-vendor offerings:
  - Copilot Studio computer use excludes sovereign clouds and has no Electron/Java/Citrix password input ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/copilot-studio-computer-use-agents-ga-deep-dive)).
  - Anthropic's local computer use is not available to Team/Enterprise, and Cowork activity is not in audit logs or the Compliance API ([Anthropic Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/14128542-let-claude-use-your-computer-in-cowork); [Claude Cowork](https://claude.com/product/cowork)).
  - Gemini computer use is still "Preview... supervise closely" ([Gemini API docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/computer-use)).
- Governance/control layer demand: Island at $6.4B ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/24/3368154/0/en/island-announces-400-million-series-f-bringing-valuation-to-6-4-billion.html)); "agent execution infrastructure" funding ~$21M to ~$504M YoY ([New Market Pitch](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/agentic-ai-funding-trends)); governance correlates with 12x more projects in production ([Databricks via Thoughtworks](https://www.thoughtworks.com/content/dam/thoughtworks/documents/e-book/State-of-AI-Agents-2026-020426.pdf)).
- Convergent competitors on "learn once, replay deterministically, heal":
  - UiPath: "deterministic or tokenless automation alongside AI" ([transcript](https://exa.ai/library/markets/stocks/PATH/earnings/FY2027/Q2/transcript)).
  - Skyvern: "compile-to-code" with self-heal ([Skyvern](https://www.skyvern.com/blog/skyvern-we-raised-2-7m-to-fix-browser-automation-open-source/)).
  - Simular: neuro-symbolic, "repeat its own success" ([Simular](https://www.simular.ai/about)).
  - Vercept's Vy had a "watch and repeat" mode before shutting down ([byteiota](https://byteiota.com/anthropic-buys-vercept-after-metas-250m-founder-poach/)).
- Vertical IT/HR/finance agent startups with human approval are raising and showing revenue (Primo €3.4M ARR; Ema $77M; Magentic, Spott) ([Sesamers](https://www.sesamers.com/funding/primo-raises-6-9m-ai-agents-corporate-it/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/23/ema-raises-77m-as-ai-starts-eating-into-enterprise-software-and-services/)).

### Inferences
- "The field is saturated" is true for the layer Decurix's architecture most resembles: desktop VM per agent plus an LLM driving it. It is less true for "prove a legacy back-office workflow runs at X% autonomy, with approval gates and a full audit, on infrastructure you control, priced per completed case". No verified revenue leader occupies that for Linux or self-hosted environments, though UiPath and AA are the obvious threats in large enterprises.
- Value is being captured where the product owns an outcome and the vendor carries deployment. It is competed away in capability layers (models, sandboxes, browsers, generic agents).
- For Decurix, the evidence favours a vertical, services-heavy wedge with outcome pricing, where its autonomy rate is the billing and trust metric. Candidates: IT ops or finance back-office on UI-only/legacy apps, or regulated or air-gapped buyers who can't use Copilot/Cowork. The evidence does not favour a horizontal "control plane for AI workers" sold as a platform against Microsoft, UiPath and hyperscaler sandboxes.
- Exit reality check: generic computer-use startups have exited as acqui-hires with the product shut down (Vercept, Rilo, Orby). That is the base-rate outcome for a horizontal play without revenue.

### Gaps
- No direct evidence found on demand or willingness to pay for self-hosted or local-model computer-use agents specifically (sovereign or air-gapped buyers). This needs primary customer discovery.
- No data on how well demonstration-learned deterministic replay performs vs pure-LLM agents in production at any vendor, beyond vendor claims.
- No verified information on Mimica, Automat, Minicor, WorkBeaver, Cyberdesk or Cua 2026 traction.

# Decurix — a16z speedrun team video (3 minutes)

Prepared on 2 November 2026 from Koustubh Rao's resume (October 2026), the YC W27 drafts and the product as built; rewritten the same day to be spoken, not read. Speedrun asks for at least half the video on the founder, the rest on product, market and traction. About 450 words at a normal pace is 3 minutes. Everything here is on the resume or in the product; the small-model service is stated as what we will offer, not as built.

## Script

### Part 1: me (about 1:30)

> Hi, I'm Koustubh. I'm the founder of Decurix.
>
> I grew up solving problems on paper. Maths olympiads, astronomy olympiads, then computer science at IIT Bombay. I like problems where you can't fake the answer.
>
> My first job was at QRT, a quant hedge fund. A year in, they gave me a team and a problem nobody wanted: the fund's market data. Years of prices, and a question that took hours to answer. I rebuilt it from the ground up. When the storage layer couldn't keep up, I wrote my own. By the time I left, the same question took under a minute.
>
> Here's what I noticed while I was doing that. AI was writing most of my code. And I was still the one reading the ticket, planning the change, running the tests, opening the pull request. Every task, the same steps. The intelligence was already there. Nobody had taught it the job.
>
> Then I'd visit my father's accounting firm. Twenty people. Bills on the desk, ERP on the screen, typing one into the other, all day. Same problem. No AI at all.
>
> Everyone is building smarter models. Almost nobody is building the thing that turns a smart model into a worker you can trust. That's what I'm building.

### Part 2: product, market, progress (about 1:30)

> Decurix is an operating system for AI workers. The idea is simple: a worker that gets smarter and cheaper every time it works. Software has never done that.
>
> A worker has its own computer. You teach it the way you'd teach a new hire: do the job once on its screen, tell it the rules. It writes them down. It asks about what it couldn't see. Then it does the next one. When it isn't sure, it stops and asks. And it never tells you it's done something it hasn't checked. That one property is what's missing from every agent demo you've seen.
>
> Every checked job becomes a trace: what the worker saw, what it did, what was right. We'll train small models on those traces in our cloud, as a paid service, so work that once needed the biggest model runs on one a fraction of the size, for a fraction of the cost, on your own machine.
>
> You bring the model. The worker, and everything it has learned, is yours. So a trained worker is something you own, and something you can sell. Anyone who knows a job teaches a worker. Anyone who needs the job done buys one. Decurix is the OS they run on.
>
> Today: a working version, twenty people using it every day at my father's firm, unpaid, and a three-minute demo that is a real run, not a staged one. Next: a business I'm not related to, paying. That's what speedrun is for.
